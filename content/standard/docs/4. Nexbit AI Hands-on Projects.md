## 4.1 Straight-Line Driving

### 4.1.1 Lesson Introduction

This lesson introduces straight-line driving with the Nexbit AI robot car. The focus is on using motor control blocks to configure the speeds of the left and right motors, enabling the robot car to move forward, pause, and reverse. Mastering motor speed parameter calibration establishes the programming foundation for subsequent motion features, such as turning and line following.

### 4.1.2 Learning Objectives

1. **Knowledge Objectives**: Identify the hardware components of a two-wheeled robot car. Understand the relationship between motor speed values and robot motion. Master the motor speed parameter range.

2. **Skill Objectives**: Complete power-on preparation for the hardware. Master adding extension packages and importing programs in MakeCode. Modify motor speed and pause duration parameters, and complete program upload and debugging.

3. **Thinking Objectives**: Understand how rotational speed differences between motors affect travel trajectories. Develop engineering thinking in parameter calibration and troubleshooting.

4. **Application Objectives**: Understand motion control principles in wheeled robotics. Recognize basic motion implementation methods for educational smart robot cars.

### 4.1.5 Expansion and Challenge

**Project 1: Adjusting Driving Speed**

Modify motor speed parameters to achieve acceleration and deceleration:
1. **Acceleration**: Increase the motor speed value, such as changing the speed to `75`, and observe the change in travel speed.
2. **Deceleration**: Decrease the motor speed value, such as changing the speed to `30`, and observe the change in travel speed.

### 4.1.6 Q&A

**Question 1: What causes the robot car to veer to one side instead of driving straight, and how is this resolved?**

Answer: Discrepancies exist in the actual rotational output between two physical motors, and uneven ground friction also affects straight-line driving. If the robot car veers to the left, slightly increase the speed of motor 1 or decrease motor 2. If the robot car veers to the right, increase motor 2 or decrease motor 1. Fine-tune parameters through multiple trials until the robot travels straight.

**Question 2: Why must the robot car be powered off before uploading the program?**

Answer: The controller executes code immediately upon upload completion. If the robot remains powered on, the motors rotate instantly and it runs forward, creating a risk of falling off the work surface and sustaining hardware damage.

**Question 3: What motion occurs when the motor speed is set to -60?**

Answer: Negative values indicate reverse rotation. When motor 1 and motor 2 are both set to `-60`, both motors rotate in reverse, causing the robot car to drive backward.

### 4.2.5 Expansion and Challenge

**Project 1: Modifying Motion Duration**

Modify delay parameters to change the duration of forward and reverse movement:
1. **Extending Motion Duration**: Increase the pause duration in milliseconds, such as changing `1,000 ms` to `2,000 ms`. The travel time lengthens, resulting in a greater travel distance.
2. **Shortening Motion Duration**: Decrease the pause duration in milliseconds. Travel time decreases, resulting in a shorter travel distance.

**Project 2: Steering via Differential Speed**

Adjust the speed difference between the two motors to observe turning effects:
1. **Turning Right**: Keep the speed of motor 2 constant and increase motor 1. For example, set motor 1 to `50` and motor 2 to `30` to steer right.
2. **Turning Left**: Keep the speed of motor 1 constant and increase motor 2. For example, set motor 1 to `30` and motor 2 to `50` to steer left.

> [!NOTE]
>
> **A larger speed differential between the two motors results in a smaller turning radius. When the differential is `0`, the robot drives straight. Rotating one motor forward and the other in reverse produces a pivot turn in place.**

### 4.2.6 Q&A

**Question 1: Why is motor 1 set to 26 and motor 2 to 30 at low speed, while motor 1 is set to 60 and motor 2 to 70 at high speed?**

Answer: Output rotational speeds differ between physical motors. Differential speed compensation is required to ensure straight travel. Motor 2 is assigned a slightly higher value to offset mechanical speed differences. Specific values require fine-tuning according to the actual drift of the robot.

**Question 2: How is the duration of forward or reverse driving modified?**

Answer: Modify the value in the `pause` block below the corresponding motor block. Larger values extend the duration. For example, changing from `1,000 ms` to `2,000 ms` increases the forward driving time from 1 second to 2 seconds. Smaller values shorten the duration.

**Question 3: What motion occurs when motor speed is set to a negative value?**

Answer: Negative values indicate reverse motor rotation. When motor 1 and motor 2 are both set to negative values, both motors rotate in reverse, driving the robot backward. Larger absolute values produce faster reverse speeds.

### 4.3.6 Q&A

**Question 1: Why does pressing button A cause motor 1 to rotate forward at `30` and motor 2 in reverse at `-20`, rather than having both rotate forward?**

Answer: Turning right requires the right wheel to rotate slower than the left wheel, or in reverse. Motor 1 drives one side forward while motor 2 drives the other side in reverse. This directional speed difference allows the robot car to turn right with a tight turning radius.

**Question 2: What is the difference between button event blocks and the `on start` block?**

Answer: The `on start` block executes automatically once when the robot powers on, serving as a hardware initialization step. In contrast, the `on button pressed` blocks are event-driven, executing embedded code only when the corresponding button is pressed, and can be triggered repeatedly.

**Question 3: How can the turning angle of the robot car be increased?**

Answer: Two methods achieve larger turning angles: first, increase the speed differential between the two motors, as greater differentials reduce the turning radius. Second, extend the pause duration of the turning action, as longer turning times produce greater angular rotation.

### 4.4.6 Q&A

**Question 1: Why does drawing a square require 4 loops instead of 1?**

Answer: A square consists of four edges and four corners. Each iteration completes one straight edge and one turn. Executing 4 iterations completes all four edges and corners, forming a closed square. Loops eliminate the need to write identical code four times, keeping the program concise.

**Question 2: What causes the robot car to trace a trapezoid rather than a square?**

Answer: Variations in straight travel distance between sides or inaccurate turning angles cause this distortion. Check whether motor speeds remain consistent, straight delays are identical, and turning delays are properly calibrated. Uneven floor friction also causes travel variations, requiring fine parameter adjustments.

**Question 3: Can the count in a `repeat` block be set to 0 or negative numbers?**

Answer: Setting the loop count to `0` prevents the code within the loop from executing, and the robot remains stationary. Negative values are typically treated as `0` and do not execute. Tracing a square requires at least `4` iterations to complete the full polygon.

Answer: The robot car must continuously monitor ground states during motion. The forever loop repeatedly executes detection and conditional logic, ensuring ongoing cliff avoidance.

## 4.16 Handlebit Controller Control

### 4.16.1 Lesson Introduction

This lesson introduces wireless communication between the Handlebit controller and the Nexbit AI robot car. The joystick on the Handlebit controller directs the robot car forward, backward, left, and right.

### 4.16.2 Learning Objectives

1. **Knowledge Objectives**: Understand the operating principles of controller wireless communication. Understand joystick signal reception and parsing logic. Master the correspondence between joystick commands and motor movements.

2. **Skill Objectives**: Master both adding extension packages and importing project files in MakeCode. Modify motor speeds and joystick deadband thresholds, and complete program pairing, upload, and debugging.

3. **Thinking Objectives**: Understand the control flow: the controller transmits signals, the robot car receives and parses commands, and the motors execute movement. Cultivate engineering thinking in wireless remote control logic and troubleshooting.

4. **Application Objectives**: Master implementation methods for wireless controller robotics. Understand practical applications of joystick remote control in competitions and hands-on maker projects.

### 4.16.4 Hands-on Project

#### (1) Project Analysis

The core project in this lesson involves controlling the Nexbit AI robot car using the Handlebit wireless controller. After power-on, the robot car completes hardware initialization and awaits a wireless connection with the Handlebit controller. Deflecting the controller joystick transmits control signals. The robot car continuously receives these signals, parses the joystick offset states, and executes forward, backward, left turn, right turn, or stop commands. The joystick outputs analog offset values, where different offsets correspond to distinct motion directions, and returning the joystick to the center position stops the robot car.

- Transmitter Complete Program and Analysis

1. **Power-On Initialization**: Upon power-on, execute `Initialize Handlebit` to initialize the controller module. Execute `radio set group` to configure the radio channel group, ensuring transmitter and receiver operate on the same communication group.

2. **Forever Loop for Continuous Joystick Detection**:
   - **Left Joystick X-axis**: When the joystick X value is less than 50, transmit number 1. When the joystick X value is greater than 200, transmit number 2. In other cases, no command is transmitted.
   - **Left Joystick Y-axis**: When the joystick Y value is less than 50, transmit number 3. When the joystick Y value is greater than 200, transmit number 4. In other cases, transmit number 5.

3. **Debounce Delay**: After each numerical transmission, add a 50 ms pause to prevent joystick jitter from sending duplicate commands excessively.

- Receiver Complete Program and Analysis

1. **Power-On Initialization**: Execute `Initialize Nexbit` to complete hardware initialization. Configure `radio set group` to match the transmitter group.

2. **Radio Receive Event**: Runs when data is received over the radio, reading `receivedNumber` and applying conditional branching:
   - `receivedNumber = 1`: Sets motor speeds, turning the robot car left.
   - `receivedNumber = 2`: Sets motor speeds, turning the robot car right.
   - `receivedNumber = 3`: Sets motor speeds, driving the robot car backward.
   - `receivedNumber = 4`: Sets motor speeds, driving the robot car forward.
   - `receivedNumber = 5`: Resets both motor speeds to 0, stopping the robot car.

### 4.16.6 Q&A

**Question 1: What causes uncontrolled drift of the robot car during joystick operation?**

Answer: Uncontrolled drift occurs when the joystick outputs slight drift values without adequate deadband thresholds configured in software. Low controller battery levels can also cause signal instability.

**Question 2: What should be inspected first if the Handlebit controller cannot control the robot car at all?**

Answer: Verify that both the robot car and controller have sufficient battery power, confirm that wireless channel pairing is complete, and verify that the program executes hardware initialization blocks.
