4.1.6 Q&A ​

Question 1: What causes the robot car to veer to one side instead of driving straight, and how is this resolved?

Answer: Discrepancies exist in the actual rotational output between two physical motors, and uneven ground friction also affects straight-line driving. If the robot car veers to the left, slightly increase the speed of motor 1 or decrease motor 2. If the robot car veers to the right, increase motor 2 or decrease motor 1. Fine-tune parameters through multiple trials until the robot travels straight.

Question 2: Why must the robot car be powered off before uploading the program?

Answer: The controller executes code immediately upon upload completion. If the robot remains powered on, the motors rotate instantly and it runs forward, creating a risk of falling off the work surface and sustaining hardware damage.

Question 3: What motion occurs when the motor speed is set to -60?

Answer: Negative values indicate reverse rotation. When motor 1 and motor 2 are both set to -60, both motors rotate in reverse, causing the robot car to drive backward.

Question 1: Why does pressing button A cause motor 1 to rotate forward at 30 and motor 2 in reverse at -20, rather than having both rotate forward?

Question 2: What is the difference between button event blocks and the on start block?

Question 3: How can the turning angle of the robot car be increased?

4.4.6 Q&A ​

Question 1: Why does drawing a square require 4 loops instead of 1?

Answer: A square consists of four edges and four corners. Each iteration completes one straight edge and one turn. Executing 4 iterations completes all four edges and corners, forming a closed square. Loops eliminate the need to write identical code four times, keeping the program concise.

Question 2: What causes the robot car to trace a trapezoid rather than a square?

Answer: Variations in straight travel distance between sides or inaccurate turning angles cause this distortion. Check whether motor speeds remain consistent, straight delays are identical, and turning delays are properly calibrated. Uneven floor friction also causes travel variations, requiring fine parameter adjustments.

Question 3: Can the count in a repeat block be set to 0 or negative numbers?

Answer: Setting the loop count to 0 prevents the code within the loop from executing, and the robot remains stationary. Negative values are typically treated as 0 and do not execute. Tracing a square requires at least 4 iterations to complete the full polygon.
